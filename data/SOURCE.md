# Data sources: what is real, what is not

## Real (read during the build)

| File | Content | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `real/dallas_county_notice_files.csv` | Count of foreclosure sale notice PDF files per city and sale month (March 62, April 32, May 4 = 98 files) as listed by the Dallas County Clerk | https://www.dallascounty.org/government/county-clerk/foreclosures.php (the page did not state the year; last-updated dates were February and March 2026) |
| `real/texas_foreclosure_rules.json` | Texas statute timing: at least 20 days to cure after a notice of default, notice of sale at least 21 days before sale, sale on the first Tuesday, 10:00 to 16:00 | Texas Property Code 51.002, https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex.%20prop.%20code%20section%2051.002 |
| `real/attom_texas_2026.csv` | July 2026 foreclosure market figures: Texas had the most foreclosure starts (3,306) and completed foreclosures (1,265) of any state; 1 in 2,653 housing units had a filing | ATTOM July 2026 foreclosure market report, https://www.attomdata.com/news/market-trends/foreclosures/july-2026-foreclosure-market-report/ ; mid-year figure via HousingWire press coverage |
| `real/zip_areas.csv` | ZIP codes for Plano, Frisco and Dallas County cities, with city labels | Assigned by the author from public knowledge of the ZIPs. Frisco straddles Collin and Denton counties and several Plano ZIPs touch Dallas or Denton, so the county label is approximate |

Caveats on the Dallas County file: each PDF the build opened held one notice, but only one file was opened, so the count of files is not a count of notices. The lists show sale months already posted, and they carry no Plano or Frisco entries because those cities sit mostly in Collin and Denton counties. Collin County posts its notices in a web application (apps2.collincountytx.gov/ForeclosureNotices) that offers no export and returned an error when read, so no Collin notices are used.

## Not real (synthetic, disclosed)

* **Every borrower, loan, payment, overdraft, credit snapshot and alternative-data signal.** There is no licensed LexisNexis, credit bureau, bank or servicer data here. The alternative-data fields (overspending index, household loan count, accounts closed, liens and judgments) are modeled on the categories a data vendor would supply, not on any vendor's actual schema. No real person or address appears anywhere in this repo.
* **The foreclosure process.** Generated by a hazard model written by the author, with timing that respects the Texas statute above. The model recovers a relationship the generator contains, so predictive scores prove the pipeline works, not that the signal exists in real servicer data.
* **Base rate.** About 2.3 percent of the synthetic loans reach a notice of sale per year (201 notices of sale among 10,000 borrowers over the 10 live months). ATTOM's Texas rate (1 in 2,653 housing units per month) is lower and covers all units, not only mortgaged ones. The synthetic book is a deliberately stressed portfolio so a small run has enough positives to measure.
* **Cut data not reachable from the sandbox:** Census ACS ZIP data, FRED, HMDA, Collin County notices and FHFA were all blocked, so none are used.
